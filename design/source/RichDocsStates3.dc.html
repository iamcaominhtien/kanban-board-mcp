ENGINEERING NOTES
What a year of weekly releases taught us
HMHoa Mai·12 Nov 2026·8 min read

When we moved from monthly to weekly releases last autumn, the first thing that changed was not the code. It was the Fridays. They became calmer than they had been in years, and nobody could quite say why until we counted the incidents.

Smaller batches, calmer Fridays

Release train, week 14: the board that replaced our monthly checklist.

A monthly release carried about sixty tickets. A weekly one carries fourteen. When something breaks, the list of suspects is short enough to read out loud in a stand-up, and the rollback is a single revert instead of an archaeology project.

We kept the same checks, the same reviewers and the same board. What went away was the release freeze, the Thursday night spreadsheet and the quiet dread.

Nobody is afraid of a release that is only forty lines long.
Tuan Vo, on the first Friday we shipped before lunch

What the numbers said

Over the year we shipped 51 releases. Rollbacks fell from nine to two, and the median time from merge to production dropped to under four hours.
