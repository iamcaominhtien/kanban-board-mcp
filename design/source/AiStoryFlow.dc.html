Screen — v1 proposal

Story, Flow by scope and Live commentary — one big job handed to the PM, and a way to look at it

The AI team now runs a whole project: a new ticket reaches the Manager, Triage picks a workflow, the PM splits a big job by feature, runs the parts and approves designs for you. In the weather-app test one request became five tickets of five to seven steps each, passed between four to six AIs. This board proposes Story, a job the PM owns from the first plan to Done; a scope bar that points the Flow tab at the whole project, one Story or one ticket; and a Live commentary that follows that scope. It then draws the screens that already run in 3.20.0 without a design. Based on main; names and tickets in the mock-ups are illustrative.
A. What this board decides
Proposed. Every row says what is decided and why; the right-hand column is the argument. Based on main at 3.20.0, not on the earlier designs.
The question
The decision
Why
What is a Story?
A fifth ticket type next to bug, feature, task and chore. It holds a goal and the criteria that say it is done, and it owns child tickets. A person hands it to the PM; the PM does the rest.
A parent ticket on main is an ordinary ticket that happens to have children. Nobody owns it, it has no state of its own and nowhere to read its progress.
Why the word Story?
Because the thing is a user story: a goal someone wants. Story point is a unit of estimate and is a different thing; the UI never abbreviates it to “points”.
The existing estimate field stays as it is. Nothing is renamed.
How deep can it go?
One level. A Story contains tickets, never Stories. A ticket the PM finds too big is promoted to a Story and its children move under it.
Flow, the board and the breadcrumb all stay readable at three levels: project, Story, ticket.
What happens to parent tickets?
They keep working. A parent ticket gets one new action, Make this a Story, which hands it to the PM. The children table and the lifecycle strip of the existing parent view become the plan section of the Story page.
No migration and no second way to do the same thing: a Story is a parent ticket that somebody owns.
Where is a Story on the board?
In the column that matches its state: Draft in Backlog, Planning in To Do, In progress and Blocked in In Progress, Review in Review, Done in Done, a dropped Story in Won’t do. A Story never sits in Testing: while any child is open, even if all the open ones are in Testing, it is still In progress. No new column.
The board has six columns (Backlog, To Do, In Progress, Review, Testing, Done) and people read it that way. Group by Story is an option, not a replacement.
Who sees a plan before it runs?
You choose per Story in the dialog: show me the plan first or the PM approves its own plan. The dialog pre-selects from the setting the project already has, autonomy.approve (who takes the design approval, in Team on its own): You pre-selects show me first, The PM pre-selects the PM approves. Same two options, no new setting.
A fixed default of show me first would stop a project that runs on its own at the first step of every Story, and each stop counts as a touch. A third option, follow Team on its own, was dropped: its risk setting measures one action and a Story or a plan has no tier to compare with it.
What does Flow filter by?
A single scope bar on top of the tab: whole project, one Story, one ticket. It drives the Team map, the Timeline and Live commentary together.
Three separate filters would drift apart. One bar means the map, the timeline and the feed always describe the same thing.
What becomes of the One ticket view?
It is the third scope. The tab stops being a sibling of the map and the timeline; it is what both of them show when you are on a ticket.
On main the Live commentary column disappears on that view. With scope it stays, narrowed to the ticket.
Does the feed filter on its own?
No. It follows the scope. The chips and the ticket box that exist today become AI filters inside the scope.
A feed with its own ticket box and a scope bar above it would be two answers to one question.
What is built first?
Flow by scope, because it works with the parent tickets that exist today. Then the Story. Then the rest of the feed. Then the screens in sections L to R.
Section S has the order and what each step needs from the code.

B. A Story from the goal to Done
A draft, four steps and one overlay. The states are the Story’s own; they are not the status of its children, and a Story is never moved by dragging.
Draft
Before step 1, a Draft. A Story is written like any ticket and waits in Backlog. Nothing runs, nothing is spent. Give it to the PM (dialog F) moves it to Planning. A Draft can also be a ticket that already has children: Make this a Story.
1Planning
PMPM
Reads the goal and the criteria, asks the BA if something is unclear, writes the plan and creates the child tickets in order, with the dependencies between them.
Moves on when the plan is approved: by you, or by the PM, as the dialog says (pre-set from who approves designs in this project).
2In progress
PMDVTSPM and the team
Child tickets run one after another or in parallel as the plan says. The PM hands out work, watches for a stall, and decides what a person would otherwise decide.
Moves on when every child ticket is Done or dropped.
3Review
PMPM
Checks each criterion of the Story against what was built, runs the demo, and writes what it found on the Story page.
Moves on when every criterion is met. A miss creates one more ticket and goes back to step 2.
4Done
YouClosed by the PM
The Story closes, the summary is written and the number of times a person stepped in is fixed.
Moves on when nothing: Done is the end. A person can reopen it.
Blocked
Blocked is not a fifth step. It sits on top of step 2 or 3 whenever the PM cannot go on without a person: an approval, a choice between two designs, a quota that ran out. It says what it is waiting for and who can unblock it, and the Story goes back to where it was the moment that is answered.
C. The Story on the board
Drawn with the six columns of the board (Backlog, To Do, In Progress, Review, Testing, Done; Won’t do is hidden as for tickets) in a window about 1650 px wide, which is what six columns at their minimum width of 200 px need. A Story card is a ticket card that reads differently at a glance: a tinted ground, a blue outline, the bookmark and the word Story where a ticket says Feature, and a progress bar where a ticket has points. It never relies on colour alone, so it still reads in the black-and-white theme. Its children carry one small chip that says which Story they belong to. A Draft waits in Backlog; Planning is in To Do; In progress and Blocked are in In Progress, also while the open children are in Testing; Review and Done as named.

D. The board grouped by Story
Group by Story is a switch in the board header, next to Filters; it is off until someone turns it on and is remembered per person. Six columns, as on the board. In a lane the Story is the header, not a card, and its children sit in the board’s own columns, so a person still reads left to right; a Story that is Done still shows its children in Done. Lanes collapse. A Draft Story has no children yet and shows as a lane header in Backlog. Tickets without a Story go to the last lane.

Open questions, with the answer this board assumes
Does a Story hold Stories?
No. One level.
Flow has three scopes; a fourth makes the breadcrumb and the cluster map unreadable. A ticket that is too big is promoted to a Story, not nested.
Can a ticket become a Story?
Yes, by the PM when it splits the work, or by a person with Make this a Story.
Its existing children move under it. Its own description becomes the goal. Nothing is copied.
Does a person approve the PM’s plan?
Per Story, pre-selected from autonomy.approve: show me first when you take design approvals, the PM approves when it does.
Main already has the setting (server/services/ai/ops.py, person or pm), so there is no new one, and a project set up to run alone does not stop on every Story. A third option that followed the risk setting was dropped: it measures one action, and a Story or a plan has no tier. A rule such as a plan of more than N tickets or a budget above X comes to you could be added later. The choice is made in the dialog (F).
Is clustering enough for hundreds of tickets, or is a heatmap needed?
Clusters first. Heatmap only if clusters are not enough.
H2 shows 31 Stories and 214 tickets in a month and stays readable because arrows fold and finished Stories fade. A heatmap answers a different question (when, not who) and the Timeline already answers it.
