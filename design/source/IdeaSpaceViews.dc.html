Idea Space — list, stages & matrix

A rebuilt Idea Space. Three ways to look at the same ideas: a ranked list for everyday use, stages to see the flow, and a matrix to choose what to test. Capture and triage, the idea page, promotion and the drawing tool are on the next boards.
A. What changes
Idea Space keeps its purpose: a safe place for rough ideas that can become tickets. What changes is how ideas get in, how you decide which to pursue, and how they leave.

B. List (the default view)
The place you open every day. Ranked by readiness so the ideas closest to being ready are on top. A row shows what you need to decide: where it is, how much of it has been tested, who wants it, and whether it already became a ticket. Warnings appear on the row, not hidden inside the idea.

C. Projects: one, all, or none
Ideas belong to a project, but you often think across projects. The switcher at the top picks what you see. All projects is for looking and triaging; No project collects ideas that are not tied to any project yet.

How it works
  • Idea Space is in the sidebar at workspace level, not inside one project. It opens on the project you were last in.
  • An idea belongs to one project, or to no project. Quick add uses the project you are in; choose “No project” if it does not fit.
  • The Inbox button counts the ideas to sort in the scope you are looking at. The sidebar badge counts all of them.
Ideas with no project
  • They can be written, tested, commented and drawn on like any idea.
  • They cannot be promoted until a project is chosen. The Promote button asks for one.
  • AI help is limited: it can read ideas and workspace Docs, but not a repository, so no effort guess.
  • Assign from the list (“Assign”), from the idea page, or while triaging.

D. Stages
The same ideas as columns. Stages follow what has been learned, not who touched it last. An idea moves to Ready when it has enough tested assumptions, to Building when you promote it, and to Shipped by itself when its ticket is Done. Archived ideas are behind the Archived filter. Dragging is still allowed, but a move that skips the checks asks first.

F. Readiness
A small, honest signal replacing the typed score.
Not startedNo assumptions written yet.
GrowingSome assumptions are tested. High-impact ones are still open.
ReadyEvery high-impact assumption is validated and none is disproved.
At riskA high-impact assumption was disproved. Rethink before promoting.
How readiness is worked out
  • Only high-impact assumptions decide it. Low-impact ones are shown but do not block. If none is high-impact, every assumption must be validated.
  • The bar in lists shows all assumptions: green validated, red disproved, grey untested.
  • It is a signal, not a rule. Ready does not promote anything by itself, and you can promote an idea that is not Ready after a confirmation.
  • An idea with no assumptions is Not started, not Ready, so a bare idea is never promoted by accident.
What an assumption has
  • A sentence: “We believe …”.
  • Impact if wrong: low, medium, high.
  • Status: untested, testing, validated, disproved.
  • Evidence: links, comments, files, tickets, drawings.
  • Optional test ticket, linked both ways. Closing the ticket asks whether the assumption is now validated or disproved.
Who sets it
  • You, or an AI member on request (see the idea page). AI suggestions are drafts that need your accept.
  • Changing status is written to the idea’s history with who and when.
