AI Team — Flow, the Timeline view

A second way to read Flow: a sequence diagram of the AI team. Each AI is a lifeline, time runs down, and every hand-off, report back, question and approval is an arrow. It shows who did what and when, where the waiting is, and what is happening now, with detail on hover and click.

Shape: lifelines as columns, time downward
Classic sequence diagram. Time runs down and the newest event is at the bottom.
A live view reads like a log: new things arrive at the bottom and the view follows. Labels on arrows stay horizontal and readable, and a day of work scrolls naturally, which a wide horizontal axis does not.
Long spans and quiet stretches
Compact time by default: periods when nothing runs and nobody waits collapse into one labelled break (“1 h 10 min with nothing running”). A Real time switch shows true proportions. A wait is never collapsed to nothing: it keeps its shape, and a very long one is drawn capped with a break glyph and its exact length.
Quiet time carries no information, waiting is the whole point. Collapsing only the first keeps the picture short without hiding a bottleneck. A strip above the diagram shows the whole range and zooms.
Whole team and one ticket
Keep One ticket as its own view, but make both draw with the same symbols and colours. The Timeline gets a scope (whole team, one ticket, a parent ticket); picking a ticket there filters the lifelines to the ones involved.
One ticket is a report (elapsed split, deliverable, completion, touches) and fits a short story on one screen. The Timeline is the order of events and scales to the whole team. Sharing symbols means nobody relearns anything; links go both ways (“See as sequence” and “Open ticket story”).
How much shows at once
Three levels. Glance: bars with a ticket key, arrows with a one-character kind badge, labels only for what matters (waits over 10 min, returns, questions). Hover: a small card. Click: a side inspector with everything and links. A Detailed switch adds step ticks and arrow labels.
The overview has to stay readable with 6 AIs and 100 events. Details are one move away, and nothing is only available on hover (keyboard focus and the inspector give the same).
Colours and symbols
The same as Team map and One ticket: AI colours on lifelines and bars; green hand-off, grey dotted report back, red dashed sent back, brown dashed returned (round 1); amber for anything that waits for you. The state dots (working, waiting for you, queued) use the same colours and the same 1.6 s pulse as the built Team map. New: purple A for Autopilot, a file glyph for Docs, a lock for permissions.
One visual language across Now, Flow and Health. Every state also has a shape or glyph, so colour is never the only signal.
Order of the lifelines
Same order as the Team map, which follows the order work usually passes between the AIs. You are at the left edge, then Autopilot, then the AIs, and Docs at the right edge.
Forward hand-offs then mostly run left to right and returns run right to left, so the two views give the same mental map. The edges keep the non-AI participants out of the way.
Narrow windows
Lifelines keep a minimum width and the diagram scrolls sideways; the headers and the time gutter stay fixed. AIs with nothing in the range collapse into one thin “N quiet AIs” strip you can open. Columns that have events are never merged.
The app runs in a resizable window. Scrolling keeps every lifeline readable; hiding the quiet ones saves the most width without losing anything.
Chat questions outside tickets
Not on the Timeline in this first version. A Board AI lifeline can be added later behind a switch.
A question typed in Ask AI is not work of the team on a ticket, so it would crowd the picture. The Timeline is about the team’s work and the people it waited on.
Sharing a range
Copy link only: it restores the range, the scope and the switches. Saving as an image comes later.
A link is only URL state, so it is cheap and covers the common need (“look at this stretch”). An image export is a separate feature.
Motion
Only things that are live move: running bars, the now line, and a hand-off that is under way, drawn as dashes that march as in the Team map (it turns solid when the next AI starts). Everything finished is still, and when the team is paused nothing moves. All motion has a still equivalent for people who ask for less movement.
Movement should say “this is happening now”. If everything moves, nothing does.
B. The Timeline, whole team, live
The second way to read Flow, next to Team map and One ticket. Each AI (and you, Autopilot and Docs) is a lifeline; time runs down; a bar on a lifeline is a run; an arrow is a hand-off, a question, an approval or a report back. Reading it top to bottom is reading what happened in order. The bottom is now: the running bars grow, the dot on the now line pulses, and the view follows it unless you scroll up. Here the BA has been waiting for an answer for 41 minutes (amber), the Tester is queued behind an offline runner (grey), and a Dev permission was decided by Autopilot (its delegate, the PM, looked at it in a short run after waiting 4 minutes) while another has waited 6 for you.

C. Reading the diagram: the symbols
Every mark has a shape or a glyph as well as a colour, and each one is the same in Team map, One ticket and here.
Lifeline
YouAutopilotPMBADesignerDevTesterReviewerDocs
One column per participant: you, Autopilot, the AIs in the Team map’s order, then Docs. The header carries the AI’s colour and a state dot (working, waiting for you, queued, offline, idle, paused).
Same colours as Team map and Now.
Run bar
Filled = finished OK. Pulsing + head = running. Red × = failed. Amber lock = blocked by a permission. Hatched amber = waiting for you. Hatched grey = queued, or waiting for a runner or another AI.
The bar’s height is its duration.
Stopped, timed out, held back
Black square: a person stopped the run midway (and a diamond on your lifeline). Clock, orange: it timed out. Pause, grey dashed: a person paused it. $, dashed amber: held back by a budget cap (it says when it resumes). Clock, hatched amber: held back by a rate limit (it says when it retries). Pause, hatched grey: held because the whole team is paused.
Section I (states) draws each one.
Hand-offs
Assigned (+), @mention (@), hand-off (›), report back (dotted ↵), returned in round 1 (brown dashed), sent back (red dashed, ×N when repeated). The slope of the arrow is the wait before the next AI started.
Kinds as recorded by the app; nothing is guessed.
You and the AIs
? an AI asks you. Lock it needs a permission. Tick your answer or approval. Red × you refused. The arrow back shows how long you took.
Questions, approvals, answers.
Autopilot
A lifeline that exists only while Autopilot is on. Its header names the delegate AI (here the PM). The delegate looks at a request in a short run, drawn as a purple-outlined bar on its own lifeline, and its decision, with the reason it wrote, is the purple A. It steps in only after a wait, within a risk ceiling and a per-hour limit, and never decides a Docs proposal, its own requests or the budget.
AI safety › Autopilot.
Docs
A page an AI created or revised is a node on the Docs lifeline: file glyph, hatched amber when changes wait for a decision.
Pages, versions, proposals, threads.
Your touches
Diamonds on your lifeline, same colours as in Flow: start (green), by rule (blue), needed (amber), avoidable (red).
Same marks as One ticket and You stepped in.
D. Detail on demand: glance, hover, click, and a Detailed switch
At a glance the diagram carries only what answers “who, when, how long, in what order”. Everything else sits one move away. Hover shows a small card; click opens a side inspector (the same content is reachable by keyboard: Tab through the marks, Enter opens, Esc closes). Detailed adds step ticks along bars and a label on every arrow, for when you are investigating one stretch.
Hover cards. Cost shows only for API-key members; a local runner shows “Not metered” (with tokens if it reports them)
