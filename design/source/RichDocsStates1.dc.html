CHAPTER THREE
The Salt Road

Nobody in Linh Dien remembered who had first called it the salt road, though everyone agreed it had never carried much salt. It ran north out of the village between two rows of drowned mangroves, and in the dry months the mud on either side cracked into plates the colour of old bread.1 Her grandmother had walked it every spring with a basket on her head and a story for each bend, and Mai had grown up believing that a road was only a long way of telling something.

By the time the ferry horn sounded across the flats she had already counted the posts twice. Forty-one, the same as yesterday, and the same as the year the floods came. The ferryman, who had once been a schoolteacher, liked to say that the village was the only place in the delta where the water kept better time than the clocks.

1 The dry season at Linh Dien runs from November to April; see the survey notes in Chapter One.
