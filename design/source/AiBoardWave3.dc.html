Board AI with your hands — risk tiers, standing orders, knowledge, GitHub and marks

Wave 3 of the AI plan (docs/specs/ai-claude-code-plan.md). The Board AI today is built on one invariant, written into its design in ten places: it only proposes drafts, and Apply is the only write. This board reverses it. The Board AI acts with your permissions, sorted by how hard a thing is to undo: looking and searching happen at once, anything reversible happens at once and keeps an Undo, and anything that cannot be walked back asks first. From there: it notices trouble on its own and can be given standing orders in plain words, the project gets a knowledge base that is searched rather than embedded, red CI and review comments on a pull request come back to the AI that wrote the code, the team can be marked against fixed test suites, and three new events join the notification design.

B. The rule being reversed
The Board AI’s design is built on one sentence: AI proposes, a person applies. It is stated ten times, and this wave replaces it. Everything else about the Board AI stays.
Where the old rule is written down
Ten places in design/screens/ai-board-assistant.md. Reversing it is a rewrite of that document, not a line change — which is why this board says what replaces each one.

What stays true
  • It still acts as you. Every write is attributed to you with through the Board AI beside it, in the activity log and in the audit chain.
  • It is still not a member. It never holds a ticket, never appears in an assignee box, and is not in the AI Team tiles.
  • Your role still binds it. A Viewer’s Board AI can look and cannot write, exactly as a Viewer cannot.
  • It still never answers for you. A question an AI asked you, and an approval, stay yours — or Autopilot’s, which is unchanged.
  • It still never handles keys, and it still cannot delete a project.
Why reverse it at all. The draft-only rule was written when the assistant was a chat box with a model that might be wrong. The thing it protects against — a confident wrong action you did not ask for — is better handled by sorting actions by how hard they are to undo, which is what a person does anyway. Asking before everything taught you to press Apply without reading, which protects nothing.
C. Three tiers, sorted by how hard a thing is to undo
Not by how important it feels. The only question for each action is whether the board can put it back by itself.
Just does it
Nothing changes, so there is nothing to undo.
  • Find a ticket, a page, a run, something in the knowledge base
  • Who is working on what, and how far along
  • Read a run, a transcript, the audit log, a schedule
  • What a Claude account has left
Does it, and keeps an Undo
Reversible, and small: up to three tickets, in one project. Above that it asks, as a bulk action.
  • Create a ticket, change its fields, move it, assign it, comment
  • Put a workflow or a review depth on a ticket
  • Start a run, stop one run, pause or resume one member
  • Create a member, give it a pack or a skill, install a plugin
  • Create or edit a workflow template, a schedule, a Docs page
Asks first
Cannot be walked back from inside the board, reaches outside it, or touches more than three tickets at once.
  • Move a ticket to Done; more than 3 tickets, or more than one project; delete anything
  • Pause all AI; change a permission, a ceiling or bypass; remove a member
  • Merge, open a pull request, push a branch
  • Budgets, accounts, settings — every change
  • Never, even if asked: enter a key or password, delete a project
What Undo actually means here
  • One action is one group, with a reverse operation per change inside it.
  • What counts as a later change: an edit by you, by any AI, by a rule, or through the MCP tools — anything that writes the same field. Reading it does not.
  • Part of the group overwritten? The rest is still undone, and the result names what it skipped and who changed it — the same way the bulk revert in ai-safety-cost E2 skips conflicts rather than forcing them.
  • What it cannot take back is listed, not hidden: a comment another AI already read and acted on, a run that already spent quota, a notification already delivered. The result says put back, but X had already happened.
  • A started run is stopped, not erased: it stays in the history with its reason.
What may never be moved between tiers
  • Only the middle tier can be lowered, and only to the first: a reversible action you are tired of seeing recorded can stop being recorded. Nothing can ever be lifted out of the last tier.
  • Fixed in the last tier, per project and for ever: moving a ticket to Done · deleting anything · Pause all AI · a permission, ceiling or bypass change · a budget or account change · merging · anything that posts outside the board.
  • Done is in that list because approving the finished work is yours (D10). Lowering it would also hand it to a standing order, and D25 says a standing order may never do an ask-first action.
  • The settings page shows the fixed ones greyed, with the reason, rather than leaving you to discover they will not move.
Why these three and not two
  • Two tiers would push everything reversible into “ask first”, which is where the app is now and why nobody reads the dialogs.
  • The middle tier is the whole point: it is where almost all the useful work is, and it is safe precisely because it is reversible.
  • The line between the middle and the last tier is one question: can the board put this back by itself? A merge cannot. A move can.
  • Anything genuinely ambiguous goes in the last tier. The cost of asking too often is annoyance; the cost of the other mistake is not.

G. Standing orders: a rule in your words, tried before it is live
The second level of acting unasked. You write what you want in a sentence; it comes back as something you can read line by line; it runs for a few days writing down what it would have done; then you turn it on.

A sentence in the Board AI chat: “if an AI hangs for more than half an hour, diagnose it and fix it if you can”.
2 · It becomes a rule you can read
When, if, do what, at most how often. You change any part of it before it is saved.
3 · It runs in trial mode
For a time you choose, it only writes down what it would have done. Nothing happens.
4 · You turn it on
After reading the trial. Every firing is recorded and can be undone.

What a rule may do
  • Only the middle tier. A rule can never do something that asks first, and cannot be made to — and the actions fixed in the last tier can never be lowered into its reach.
  • It cannot do what the PM is doing on the same ticket, and two rules cannot set each other off.
  • It respects quiet hours, and Pause all AI stops every rule as well as every AI.
  • Each firing is one Undo group, exactly like a Board AI action, and sits in the rule’s own history.
Rules that come ready-made
  • An AI hangs → diagnose, then act on the diagnosis.
  • A run failed on the network → try once more at a higher effort.
  • CI went red on a pull request the board opened → give it back to the Dev who wrote it.
  • A ticket has sat in Review for 24 hours → remind me once.
  • An account is near its window → hold the work that is not urgent.
  • All off until you turn them on, and all start in trial mode.

How an AI actually finds something
Table of contents
One line per document. It reads this first, and often that is enough to know which file it wants.
Keyword search
BM25 over the text, the summaries and the keywords. It tries more than one wording, the way it does with code.
Read
It opens the part it needs, not the whole file.
Three MCP tools: knowledge_index, knowledge_search, knowledge_read. Results say which store they came from.
Who sees it, and what it is allowed to say
  • A document is only as visible as its source. A Docs page that is private stays private: it is indexed for the people and AIs that could already open it, and never becomes reachable through the shared store.
  • The shared store is for things meant to be shared — company conventions, a security review. Adding a project document to it is a deliberate move and says who will be able to read it.
  • An uploaded file is untrusted input. Its text is fenced and labelled with its source before any AI reads it, the same way a guest comment is in ai-safety-cost F2, so a line inside a PDF cannot act as an instruction.
  • Permissions are still enforced outside the model: a document cannot widen what an AI is allowed to do, whatever it says.
Why not embeddings
  • You did not want documents going to an embedding service, and a local model is slow enough to be felt on every lookup.
  • Claude reads these documents anyway when it works, so sending them to one more service buys nothing and adds a key.
  • For a store this size, a table of contents plus BM25 is what a person would do, and it is explainable when it goes wrong.
  • It stays an option. If a store grows past what keyword search can handle, embeddings become a switch — not a rewrite.

M. The states these screens have to have
Most of these exist because the Board AI now does things. A thing that was done needs a way of being seen, refused, taken back and explained.
Undo is too late
Something else changed the same field. The button says what overwrote it and when, and offers to open that change rather than failing silently.
It did half of it
Three of five changes went through and the fourth was refused. It says which, why, and offers to undo the three.
Your role will not allow it
A Viewer’s Board AI says “I can look this up but I cannot change it — your role is Viewer”, and does not draft it either.
A rule in trial mode
Shown everywhere as trial, including on the cards it would have acted on. Nothing it writes down looks like something that happened.
A rule stopped itself
It hit its own limit. The row says so, with the time, and nothing fires again until you look.
Two rules would loop
Refused when you save the second one, naming the loop. Not discovered at three in the morning.
The knowledge base is empty
A new project. “Nothing here yet. Add a file and the AIs can look things up in it.” Not an error.
A file could not be read
A scanned PDF with no text layer. It says so on the row and offers to run it through a model once, rather than silently indexing nothing.
GitHub is unreachable
The polling says last checked 20 minutes ago instead of pretending CI is green. No rule fires on stale data.
A suite has never run
No score and no trend line, just never run and a Run button. No invented baseline.
Everything is paused
Pause all AI stops the rules too, and every rule row says paused with everything else rather than looking broken.
The Board AI is off
Turning the feature off leaves the chat and the cards gone, the rules paused and listed as such, and nothing half-running.
N. Where the data comes from, what is decided, and what is still open
The table is for whoever builds it. The two cards below separate what the design has settled from what only the build can answer — and this is the wave with the most of the second kind, because it is the one that changes a rule the app was built on.
Where it comes from
Notes
A Board AI action
New: what it did, as a group, with one reverse operation and who it was recorded as.
Written to the activity log and the audit chain as you, through the Board AI. The group is the unit of Undo.
The tier of an action
New, as configuration: every action the Board AI can take, in one of three tiers, with a locked flag.
One table, in the code and shown in the settings. A locked action cannot be moved out of the ask-first tier by anyone, and the server refuses it — which is also what stops a standing order reaching one.
A signal
New: computed by code on a timer. Nothing is stored except which cards you dismissed or snoozed.
The five board insights already work this way; these six join them.
A standing order
New: the plain sentence you wrote, the structured rule, its limits, its state (off, trial, on).
A rule may only name actions in the middle tier; the server refuses one that names anything else.
A firing
New: what set it off with its numbers, what it did (or would have done), and the Undo group.
A trial firing is the same record with nothing applied, marked would have.
A knowledge document
New: the text, a one-line summary, keywords, which store, and when it was read last.
Everything becomes text on upload; an image gets a description written once by a light model. No embeddings.
The knowledge index
New: FTS5 over the text, the summaries and the keywords. Docs already has FTS.
Three MCP tools read it. Results say which store they came from.
A watched pull request
New: the ticket, the pull request number, the last state seen, and when it was last checked.
Polled with gh while it is open. Nothing is stored about a pull request the board did not open.
A mark
New: suite, when, which model, the score, and the run behind it.
On the sandbox project, so these runs never appear in a real project’s history or quota reports.
The three events
Reuses the notification log exactly as it is.
No new machinery; the grouping and retention already apply.
Decided before building
  • The tier is decided by one question: can the board put it back by itself? Not by how important it feels.
  • A fixed set can never leave the ask-first tier — Done, deleting, Pause all, permission, ceiling and budget changes, merging, and anything that posts outside the board. Only the middle tier can be lowered.
  • More than three tickets, or more than one project, is a bulk action and asks first, with the list shown.
  • Everything it does is attributed to you, with through the Board AI beside it, and your role still binds it.
  • It still never answers a question meant for you and never approves on your behalf. That is Autopilot’s job and it is unchanged.
  • A rule may only do reversible things, and the server enforces that rather than trusting the editor.
  • A new rule cannot go live until its trial has run — one firing, or a day with nothing to fire on. A trial firing can never be mistaken for a real one.
  • Every rule has the same shape: notice → diagnose → act, with let the AI analyse, then decide offered beside a fixed action, and the option to write what it learned into the project memory.
  • Signals cost no quota. The only model call among them is the hang diagnosis, which happens anyway.
  • No embeddings and no third service for the knowledge base. Keyword search plus a table of contents, with embeddings left as a later switch.
  • Only pull requests the board opened, and a failure is checked against the base branch before anyone is blamed. On such a pull request, pushing to its branch and replying on its threads happen inside the run without asking, because a fix that cannot be pushed is not a fix; everything else that leaves the board still asks.
  • Marks run on a sandbox, because a score is only comparable if the questions do not change.
  • Pause all AI pauses the rules too. One switch, no exceptions.
What the build still has to settle
  • How far Undo really reaches. A field is easy. A started run that already posted a comment another AI read is not, and the design admits it rather than hiding it. Where exactly the honest line falls is a build question.
  • Whether a sentence is enough to write a rule. If the structured rule comes back wrong often, the editor becomes the main way in and the sentence becomes a shortcut.
  • How often to poll GitHub. Often enough to be useful, rarely enough not to spend an API budget on an idle pull request. Start at five minutes while one is open.
  • Whether keyword search is enough. It is the right default for a store of tens of documents. At hundreds, the switch to embeddings may have to be made rather than offered.
  • What a mark is worth. Seven suites on a sandbox measure a sandbox. The trend is the useful part, and it only becomes useful after a couple of months of data.
  • How a person tells the two apart in practice. D24 settles who the coordinator is — a PM member, with the Board AI above it — but on a busy day the difference between “the PM decided” and “the Board AI overrode it” has to be readable on the ticket at a glance. Wave 2 section H records both; whether that is enough is a build question.
