Why we moved our roadmap into the board
An Nguyen · 6 min read

We kept the roadmap in a slide deck for two years. Every quarter someone exported it, every sprint someone forgot to update it, and by the third week nobody trusted it. Moving it into the board did not make planning easier, but it made the answer to “what are we building?” the same for everybody.

The first month was the hard one. Tickets had to be small enough to finish and big enough to matter, and each epic needed a page that explained the why. By the second month the pages were doing the work the meetings used to do.

What surprised us most was how little the tooling mattered once the habit was there. A shared place to write things down, and a promise to read it before asking.

Tickets stayed small, pages stayed short, and the board became the place we wrote things down.

Nobody had warned her that the quiet would be the hardest part. The wind she could bear, and the cold, and even the dark that came down at four in the afternoon; but the silence had a weight of its own, as if the whole valley were holding its breath and waiting for her to speak first.
