AI Team — where people stepped in, and whether the AI is healthy

The second half of AI Team: every manual touch with why it was needed, a health page with five measures and a list of what to fix, where the view shows up in the app, its states, and the definitions behind every number.
A. You stepped in: every manual touch, and which ones were needed
Every time a person had to act, in order, with the AI and ticket it was about. Each touch carries one of three marks. By rule: the project is set up to ask for it (approvals, Done). Needed: only a person had the information or judgement. Avoidable: a setting, a note in the instructions or an automatic retry would have removed it. These marks are the answer to “how autonomous is the team”, and each one says why. Click a row to open the run, the approval, the Docs page or the audit entry behind it. When a tool acts for a person through MCP (Settings › Integrations, full control), the touch shows a via MCP mark beside the person. It counts as that person’s touch and can be filtered, so a person’s own touch and a tool’s touch on their behalf can be told apart.

How a touch gets its mark
  • By rule when it answers an approval the project asked for, or a move above the AI’s ceiling. Never counted against the team.
  • Avoidable when the same question got the same answer 3 or more times, when a manual re-run followed a rate limit or a timeout, or when a settings change fixed a recurring failure.
  • Needed for everything else: answers, edits and stops where a person knew something the AI could not.
  • In Docs, accepting or rejecting a proposal is by rule when the project asks for approval of doc edits; resolving a review thread is needed, since only a person can close it.
  • The marks are rules you can read in the definitions (section E), not a judgement of the person.
B. Health: is the AI feature working?
One page that says whether the AI team is healthy, in plain words first, then in numbers. Five measures: reliability (does it run), autonomy (how much does it do alone), speed, quality (how often work is sent back) and cost. Honest numbers
  • These measure how the setup works, not how smart an AI is. A by-rule touch is never held against the team.
  • Every tile says how many runs it rests on. Under 10 runs there is no verdict; under 20 a change is called “too few to be sure”.
  • Thresholds are defaults. A project can change them in Settings › AI.
  • The same range and the same AIs always give the same numbers. Nothing is sampled.
Who sees what
  • Everyone in the project can open AI Team. Viewers see it without the buttons.
  • People are shown by name in touches, because the audit log already does. Export to CSV follows the audit log’s permissions.
  • Pages and tickets a person cannot see are not shown in evidence.
What the app needs, and a build order
  • Hand-off kinds are exact for runs (trigger: assign, mention, handoff, report_back, schedule, and the chain). Assignments and status moves still come from the activity log.
  • To record when the action happens, not derive later: an origin on audit and activity entries (the via MCP mark), and who answered a question.
  • Docs: page versions, review threads (open, resolved, orphaned) and proposals, read for the journey and for Needs you. Parent tickets: roll up the children and their holders.
  • Compute the numbers on the server: one aggregate endpoint per tab, with the range. A run’s step list is large and is never read by the client. Live updates reuse the board invalidation event.
  • Build order, each step useful alone: Now, then Flow for one ticket, You stepped in, Health, then the entry points (top bar badge, ticket journey line).
