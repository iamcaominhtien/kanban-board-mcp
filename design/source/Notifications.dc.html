Notifications — one place for what needs you and what happened

Today every part of the app toasts in its own way and nothing is kept. This board proposes one notification centre: a bell and a panel, an all-projects page, two kinds of notification (needs you, for information), grouping, notifications that close themselves when the work is done elsewhere, a quieter use of toasts, the desktop behaviour, following and muting, the settings page, and a card that sums up the day.
A. The decisions, in one table
What the rest of the board follows. Open points are in section N.
QuestionDecisionWhy
What it is
One notification centre for the whole app: a bell in the top bar with a panel, and an All notifications page for every project. Every notification is kept, with read and unread, until the retention setting removes it. Clearing a row is a state, not a delete (section L).
A toast that disappears is not a feature. Anything worth telling someone is worth keeping.
Two kinds
Needs you: the app cannot continue without you (a question, a failure, a signed-out account, an @mention). For information: it happened, read it when you like.
The first may interrupt, including on the desktop. The second never interrupts; it only adds to the list.
What a toast is for now
Only three things: a Needs you event while you are looking, a failure of something you just did, and an Undo offer. Everything else goes to the panel quietly. At most 3 toasts at a time, oldest collapses into “+5 more”.
The pile in the screenshot above is eight “for information” toasts. None of them needed the screen.
Action feedback is not a notification
“Saved”, “Copied”, “Moved to Done” become a small grey line at the bottom of the window that fades after 2 s, with no icon and no close button. They are never kept and never counted.
They answer “did my click work”, which is a different question from “what happened while I was away”.
It closes itself
A Needs you item is tied to the thing it is about. Answer the team in the ticket, sign the account back in — and the notification is Done by itself, wherever you did it.
A list you have to tidy by hand becomes a list nobody reads.
Grouping
Events of the same kind, near in time, become one row with a count: “8 AI finished work”, expandable. The desktop gets one notification for the group, not eight.
The common case in this app is many AI teams finishing at once.
Who gets one
A notification is addressed to a recipient, which is a person or an AI team: an AI can be @mentioned, or wait on a run that has finished. Read and done are kept per recipient. The screens in this board are the person’s; an AI reads its own through the board, not through this panel.
The brief says an AI is both a cause and a recipient. Only telling an external AI over MCP is out of this design, because it has no screen — but the data must not have to be redone for it.
All projects
The panel and the page show every project you can see, with a project chip on each row and a filter. Web and desktop show the same list, because it lives on the server.
One person works across several boards; notifications should not hide behind a project switch.
Follow and mute
Follow or stop following a ticket, a Docs page or a whole project; switch off kinds you do not care about. What you ask for, what you are mentioned in and what you started, you follow by default.
The only way to keep a list quiet is to let people leave the parts that do not concern them.
A day in one card
At the end of the day, one Today card in the panel: what finished, what failed, what is still waiting, what it cost. In the app only.
Catching up should take one card, not thirty rows.
Where it does not go
No email, no push to a phone, no sound by default. The desktop app may show an OS notification and a badge; the web tab shows the title badge only.
This is a local-first app for one person per board. Email is a different feature.

Left: what the app does now, drawn from its own code — one toast provider with no limit, and five features that each notify in their own way. Right: what this design puts in its place.

Reading and clearing
  • A row is read when you open the panel and it has been on screen for a moment, or when you open what it points at.
  • Mark all read clears the count, not the list.
  • The x on hover clears a row: it leaves the panel and stays on the All notifications page under Cleared.
  • A Needs you row is not cleared that way. It is resolved (by doing the work, or by Dismiss where the event offers one), or snoozed.
  • The panel shows the last 30 days; the page shows everything still kept. One setting decides when a notification is deleted for good (section L).
The count
  • The red number is Needs you only, so it means “this many things wait for me” and nothing else.
  • Unread updates show as a blue dot with no number.
  • The same count is on the desktop icon and in the web tab title.
  • Opening the panel does not clear the red number: resolving the work does.

When two events become one row
  • Same kind, same project, and inside the same 10 minute window.
  • The row keeps counting while the window is open: “6 AI finished” becomes “8 AI finished” in place, and the row returns to the top.
  • The desktop gets one notification for the group, when the window closes, not one per event.
  • Needs you items group only when they are the same request from the same AI (a question asked again), never across tickets: each one needs its own decision.
  • A group is read as a whole; opening one line inside it does not read the rest.
  • Over 20 in one window the row says “20+” and the page lists them all.
F. A notification that closes itself
F1. Answer in the ticket, and the notification closes itself
A Needs you item is a view of a real thing: a run waiting for an answer, an account. The notification follows that thing, so the list is never a second to-do list to tidy.
The team asks
Its run pauses. The row appears in Needs you, a toast shows if you are looking, and the desktop is told.
You answer in the ticket
Or in the chat banner, or from the toast. Nothing about the notification itself.
The row goes grey and says why
Within a second, in every open window. The red count drops. The row stays until the end of the day, then only on the All notifications page.
F2. What counts as handled
The rule is the same everywhere: the notification is done when the underlying thing no longer needs a person. Looking at it only marks it read — reading and resolving are two different things.
A question
You answer the run, anywhere — ticket, chat banner, the toast, or someone else answers for you.
An account signed out
The account signs in again. One notification for the account closes for every team on it.
A failed run
You press Retry and the new run starts, or you dismiss it.
A mention
You reply, or you dismiss it. Opening the comment only marks it read: an AI that asked for a decision is still blocked until you answer.
A budget cap
The cap is raised, or the day rolls over.

Rules
  • At most 3 toasts. A fourth collapses the oldest into “+N more”, which opens the panel.
  • A toast for an event that is already resolved closes itself.
  • Hovering a toast stops its timer; it never covers the bottom-right card of a dialog.
  • The quiet line has no icon, no close and no history. It never appears for anything another person or an AI did.
  • Nothing makes a sound unless you switch sound on.

When and what
  • Written at 18:00 by default, on the days you choose (weekdays to start). The time is a setting.
  • Only what happened since the last card, so nothing is counted twice.
  • If the app was closed all day it is still there when you open it, with the date.
  • A quiet day says so in one line instead of showing empty numbers.
  • It is never a Needs you item: it is a summary, not work.
How it differs from Board health
  • Board health (09:00, Board AI) is an AI reading the board: stuck tickets, WIP, risk. It costs tokens and gives advice.
  • This card (18:00) is the app counting what happened to you today: runs, failures, waiting, spend.
  • They link to each other and never repeat each other’s lines.
  • If the Board AI is off, this card is unaffected.
  • There is a third digest today, and it goes: the app holds its quiet events (digest = true) and releases them at 09:00 as one “digest” row. Those events become ordinary Updates rows, shown when they happen, and the day card is the only summary of them. Without this, widening the table would hide every quiet row until the next morning.

Rules on the desktop
  • Clicking the notification brings the window up at the thing itself — the ticket, the account, the run — not at the panel.
  • While the window is focused and showing the thing, no desktop notification: a toast is enough.
  • At most one every 20 seconds; anything beyond that waits and becomes one grouped notice.

What a switch off actually does
  • In app off: no row in the panel and nothing in the count. The event is still recorded and still appears on the All notifications page and in the day card.
  • Desktop off: no notification from the operating system. Nothing else changes.
  • Both off: it still happened, and you can still find it. This is a change from today: the old switch records nothing when both are off, so the event is lost for good.
  • Muting a ticket, page or project works the same way: quiet, not erased.

What is shared and what is not
  • Read, done and snoozed are on the server, so a row read in one window greys in the other within a second.
  • Toasts are per window and only in the window you are looking at — never in all of them.
  • The desktop notification is sent once, by the app, not once per window.
N. Where the data comes from, what is decided and what is left
Most of this exists in some form: a notification table, an event stream, a follow table, an inbox and six feature-specific hooks. The new piece is an event log underneath them: one append-only record with increasing ids that the panel, the desktop app and (later) an external AI waiting on the board all read.
Piece
Holds
Where it comes from
Event log
one append-only row per thing that happened: project, kind, what it points at, who caused it, the facts, and an increasing id
this is the new piece. The schedules feed already works this way (integer ids, a client that resumes from the last one it saw). It is the source for everything below, and for the planned MCP wait tool and kanban wait command, so an external AI can resume from where it stopped instead of looping on sleep.
Notification
one per recipient per event: read, done and why, snoozed until, group id. A recipient is a person or an AI team.
built from the log, not written beside it. AiNotification is the closest thing today (title, body, ref_kind, ref_id, member_id, people, urgent, inapp, desktop, digest) but it is one row for everyone, with a people list and a role check in visible_notifications.
What writes to the log
a run changes state, a team asks a question, a page is published, a comment is posted, a schedule slot ends, an account signs out, a cap is reached, memory restarts or stops a run
today six UI hooks each watch their own data and toast: runs, schedules, Docs publishes, Docs comments, AI accounts and AI pauses. They move to the server, so a closed window misses nothing.
Delivery to open windows
the panel, the count, the toasts
the shared SSE stream at /events carries one more message: “the log moved to id N”. The window asks for what it has not seen, so a dropped message costs nothing. /events is in memory in one process, which is why the log, not the stream, is the source.
Delivery to the desktop
the OS notification and the number on the icon
new work in the Electron main process: it follows the log itself and owns both. Today the only desktop notification is new Notification() from the page, so nothing arrives with the window closed and two windows would send two.
Follow and mute
per ticket, page, project and event kind, per recipient
DocsFollow already stores who follows a project’s Docs space, and the browser keeps the toggle. That becomes one case of this table.
The day card
counts and lines for one day, per recipient
read from the log for that day. Nothing is asked of an AI, so it costs nothing and cannot invent.
Decided before building (issue #253)
  • The name “Needs you” is kept for both the panel tab and the team page. The same items appear in both, and resolving in either closes both.
  • Each recipient sees their own rows. So that nothing an owner or admin sees today is lost, they are made recipients of every AI event.
  • The log is kept as long as the notifications, under the one retention setting (90 days). A feed or wait cursor older than the oldest kept event is answered, not ignored: cursor_too_old: true with the current head id, meaning “start from now”.
  • The day card is written on weekdays at 18:00, and the time is a setting.
  • Settings › Notifications is its own page under App settings.
What the build still has to decide
  • The log comes first. The panel, the desktop app and the planned wait tool all read one log. Building the panel on a notification table alone would mean adding the log underneath it later.
  • Recipients include AI teams in the data from the start, with read and done per recipient, even though no screen shows an AI’s list in v1. Telling an external AI over MCP is the wait tool’s job, not this design’s.
  • A notifier in the Electron main process is new work, not an existing capability: the OS notification, the dock or taskbar number, the click that opens the right thing, and one notification per machine rather than per window.
  • Six notifiers move in the first slice, not five: runs, schedules, Docs publishes, Docs comments, AI accounts and AI pauses. Leaving the last one out means every question and budget event arrives twice, old toast and new row.
  • The 09:00 digest goes. Its quiet events become ordinary Updates rows and the 18:00 day card is the only summary. Otherwise widening the table hides every quiet row until the next morning, and the person has three summaries.
  • The switches move from per project to app-wide (AiProjectSettings.notify holds them today, for eight events only), with per-project mutes as the exception, and In app locked on for everything that needs you.
  • Off means quiet, not unrecorded. Today the old switch writes nothing when in-app and desktop are both off. This design keeps the row so the page and the day card stay complete; that is a change to make deliberately.
  • Retention is one setting and it is the only thing that deletes: the panel showing 30 days is a view, clearing a row is a state, and the audit log is unaffected.
  • Board health (09:00) stays separate and keeps its own digest; it reads the board, this card counts what happened to you.
  • Sound is off and is not designed beyond one switch.
  • Mentions by people are designed for, but today only AI teams can mention the single human on a board.
  • Desktop notification actions (Answer, Retry) assume Electron buttons on macOS and Windows. Where the system does not show them, clicking the body opens the thing.
