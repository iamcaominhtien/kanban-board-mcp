AI Team — Flow, the Timeline view: time, scope, motion and states

The details behind the Timeline: how long and quiet stretches are drawn, how one ticket and the whole team share one picture, what moves while it is live, every state, and where the data comes from.
E. Long spans and quiet stretches
Time is the whole diagram, so how it is stretched matters. The rule is simple: compress what carries no information (quiet time), and keep what does (waiting). Five pieces make that work.
1. Quiet stretches collapse
A stretch longer than 10 minutes with no run active and nobody waiting becomes one labelled break. The gutter keeps the real clock on both sides. Double-click a break to open it at true scale.
2. A long wait is shown, never hidden
A wait keeps its hatched bar even when long. Over 60 minutes it is capped (at most about 150 px) with a break glyph and its exact length; the colour gets stronger the longer it is (over 10 min amber, over 60 min red outline). That is the bottleneck, so it is the last thing to compress.

The rules
  • Collapse only when nothing runs and nobody waits for more than 10 minutes. A wait, a queue and a run are never collapsed.
  • Cap a wait over 60 minutes at about 150 px, with its exact length written on it.
  • Real time never collapses anything; it just gets tall, so it scrolls and the strip stays on screen.
  • A range over one day starts as bands (5); one day or less starts as the diagram.
  • The gutter always shows the real clock, including on both sides of a break.
F. Whole team, one ticket, and a ticket that kept coming back
The Timeline has a scope. Choosing one ticket (or a parent and its children) keeps only the lifelines that took part, so a ticket’s story is the same picture, just filtered. One ticket stays as its own view for the summary it carries (elapsed split, deliverable, completion, touches); the two link to each other. G. What moves, and when
Only what is live moves. The Timeline is open for long stretches, so motion is small, slow and tied to meaning: “this is running”, “this just arrived”, “this is waiting for you”. Everything finished is still. The demo on the left is a real animation; the four frames show one hand-off in order. A hand-off under way marches as dashes, exactly like the Team map, so the two views move the same way.

Budget for motion
  • Animate only what is on screen and only the last 5 minutes of it; older rows are still.
  • Stop everything while the tab is hidden, and resume without replaying.
  • At most about 20 things animate at once; beyond that the rest are still.
  • While the whole team is paused nothing animates.
  • The reduced-motion setting turns every loop and every travel into its still picture.

I. States
Loading
Lifelines appear first, then the bars; the header and the toolbar are usable while it loads. Slow after 3 s: “Still loading the last 24 hours”.
Nothing in this range
No AI work between 08:00 and now
The lifelines stay so you can see who is on the team, all idle. A line says when the last run ended.
Lots of events
Close events between the same two lifelines group into one arrow with ×N; click to expand them. Steps are ticks, shown only in Detailed. The diagram draws only the visible rows (virtualised) and the strip shows where the dense parts are.
Something is running now
The bars grow, the now line pulses, and Following live is on. Scroll up and it turns into “Paused: 3 new events ↓”.
Errors: a run fails, a permission blocks
A failed run is a red bar ending in × (hover: the reason, and whether it retried). A blocked run is an amber bar ending in a lock, with a red arrow to you for each refusal (×3). Both also appear as cards in Needs you.
Sent back again and again
The loop is boxed and labelled “ping-pong: sent back 3 times” (section F). The count on each arrow goes up, the box turns red at 3, and a card appears in Health › What to fix.
Waiting for a person
Hatched amber bar with the wait written on it, a pulsing “?” on your lifeline, and the card in Needs you. Over 60 minutes it is capped with a break glyph (section E).
Data is behind
Showing events up to 10:14Can’t reach the server. The now line stops, nothing animates, and it catches up by itself.
No Autopilot, or no Docs work
The Autopilot lifeline only exists in a project that has Autopilot, and the Docs lifeline only when an AI touched a page in the range. Otherwise the columns are not drawn, so there is no empty lifeline.
Only one AI, or a viewer
One AI: you and that AI, still useful for its waits. A viewer sees everything but the Answer, Approve and Open-in-run buttons are hidden, and the inspector says why.
Not metered
A run on a local runner (Claude Code, Antigravity) is not metered, so its hover card and inspector say Not metered instead of an amount, and show tokens when the runner reports them (new and cached apart, or input per call). Only API-key members show a cost, and the team cost says how many AIs are left out.

What the app needs
  • One read endpoint for the Timeline: the range and the scope as parameters, answering with a flat list of events (kind, time, from, to, ticket, run, duration, state) already grouped when they are very close. The client builds the axis, the breaks and the caps.
  • Pagination by time window, newest first when following live; never one big response.
  • Live updates should come as typed events (what changed), not through the board-wide invalidation that makes every query refetch: with several AIs running that is the main load problem, and the Timeline would add to it. It asks for events after the last one received.
  • A run’s step list is not part of the Timeline response; it is fetched when you open the inspector (or turn Detailed on for a visible stretch). A step’s time is stored as hour:minute:second without a date, so step ticks across midnight need a stored date.
Reading it without a mouse or without colour
  • A Table toggle lists the same events in order (time, from, to, kind, ticket, duration) for screen readers and for copying.
  • Every mark can be reached with Tab; Enter opens the inspector, Esc closes it, arrow keys move between neighbouring events.
  • Every state has a shape or a glyph as well as a colour (lock, ×, ?, hatch).
  • Times are shown in the person’s time zone, with the date on each midnight line.
Settled by the code, and still open
  • Settled: Autopilot decisions are recorded (who, when, reason, path, and an autopilot_decision audit entry); who answered a question and by which path is stored; the origin of an action (via MCP) is recorded when it happens.
  • Settled: the review-round signal for returned and sent back, as defined for Health.
  • Open: a cap on events per window (about 500), beyond which groups are shown instead of single arrows.
  • Open: Docs proposals exist only in projects that hold AI edits for review (and Autopilot never decides them), so the Docs lifeline’s proposal nodes appear only there.
  • Open: a stored date on steps, for the Detailed step ticks.
  • Open: a parent ticket can have a dozen report-back arrows to one AI in an hour; they group into one arrow with ×N (see states) and the cap above applies.
  • Open: pause and resume must be typed live events, and paused time is kept apart from waiting.
  • Open: the share link carries the range, the scope and the switches in the URL.
